China Combo Mortgage: How Provident Fund and Commercial Loans Combine

A "combo" mortgage (组合贷) splits one home purchase into two loans priced at two different rates: a subsidised housing provident fund loan and a market-rate commercial loan. The blended payment is lower than an all-commercial loan — but only by a specific, calculable amount.

1. What a combo loan actually is

China's housing provident fund (住房公积金) is a compulsory savings scheme. Employees and employers each contribute a percentage of salary into an individual account, and that account entitles the holder to borrow at a subsidised rate for a home purchase.

The catch is that the fund loan is capped. The cap varies by city, by contribution history, and by whether you are a first-time buyer — commonly between ¥400,000 and ¥1,200,000. If your purchase needs more than the cap, the remainder is funded by a commercial bank loan at market rate. Two loans, one property, one monthly payment that is the sum of both.

2. Two loans, two rates

The two portions never blend into a single average rate. Each amortises independently over its own term, and the total monthly payment is simply:

Total monthly payment = fund portion payment + commercial portion payment

8. How the provident fund portion is capped

The size of the fund portion is not a choice — it is set by your city, and it is usually the binding constraint on how much a combo loan can save you. Four inputs typically matter:

  • Account balance. Many cities cap the loan at a multiple of the balance in your provident fund account.
  • Contribution history. A minimum number of months of continuous contribution is almost always required.
  • City ceiling. An absolute maximum per borrower or per household, which varies widely between cities.
  • Property type and purchase count. Second homes usually face a lower ceiling and a higher rate.

Because the cap, not the rate, drives the saving, the first question to ask is not "what is the fund rate" but "how much can I actually borrow through the fund".

Moving the fund share from 30% to 80% nearly triples the monthly saving. A city that permits a larger fund loan delivers far more benefit than a 0.1 percentage point cut in the fund rate.

11. Which portion to prepay

When you make an early repayment on a combo loan you must choose where it goes. The rule follows directly from the rates:

  • Pay the commercial portion first. At 3.5% versus 2.6%, every yuan retired commercially saves roughly 35% more interest per yuan.
  • Ask whether each portion can be re-amortised separately. Some lenders shorten the term on the portion you prepaid; others simply reduce the payment. Ask which, because the two produce very different totals.
  • Check whether the city allows cross-portion prepayment. In some cities you cannot freely move money between the two loans.

12. Where combo loans cause trouble

  • Two approvals, two timelines. The fund centre and the bank assess separately, and completion frequently takes longer than a single commercial loan.
  • Term alignment. The two portions may carry different maximum terms, which complicates the combined schedule.
  • Second-home rates. The fund rate rises to 3.075% above five years for a second home, narrowing the advantage considerably.
  • Balance requirements in retirement. Some cities require the fund loan to be cleared before the borrower retires, shortening the effective term.
  • Seller acceptance. Longer approval timelines can be a disadvantage in competitive purchases.

13. Questions to ask before applying

  • What is the maximum fund loan for my city, my contribution history and this property type?
  • Is this purchase counted as first or second home for fund purposes, and at what rate?
  • Can the two portions carry the same term, and can I prepay either one separately?
  • How long does combined approval usually take, and does the seller's timeline allow it?
  • Does the 2026 purchase-loan interest subsidy apply, and does it interact with the fund portion?

The answers determine the saving far more precisely than any online average does.

4. Why the saving is smaller than people expect

Most buyers assume a "lower rate" means a much lower payment. The arithmetic says otherwise: moving ¥300,000 from 3.5% to 2.6% is a 0.9 percentage point improvement on 30% of the loan. Spread across the whole ¥1,000,000, the effective rate drops by only about 0.27 points.

This is why the fund cap matters more than the fund rate. A city that allows ¥800,000 of fund borrowing produces a far larger saving than a 0.1% rate cut.

5. Prepaying a combo loan

When you make an early repayment on a combo loan, you must decide which portion to apply it to. The rule is straightforward:

  • Pay the commercial portion first. It carries the higher rate, so every yuan retired there saves more interest per yuan than the fund portion.
  • Check whether your city allows re-amortising each portion separately. Some lenders shorten the term on the portion you prepaid; others reduce both proportionally.
  • Fund loans sometimes have early-repayment restrictions or require the fund account balance to be used first.

Before committing, run the numbers both ways — shortening the term usually beats reducing the payment, and the gap widens the earlier you are in the schedule.

6. When a combo loan is the wrong choice

  • You plan to sell within a few years. The interest saved in years one to three is small; the extra paperwork and possible restrictions are not worth it.
  • The fund application delays settlement. Fund loan approval takes longer than commercial approval. If the seller has a tight deadline, this is a real risk.
  • Your fund balance is needed elsewhere. In some cities the fund can also be withdrawn for rent or major medical expenses.

7. How to work out your own number

You need four inputs: total loan, fund cap in your city, fund rate, and commercial rate. Split the total into the two portions, price each independently, then add. Any calculator that applies a single blended rate to the whole balance will give you the wrong answer.
