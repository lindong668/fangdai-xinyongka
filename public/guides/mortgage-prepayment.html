Early Mortgage Repayment: Shorten the Term or Reduce the Payment?

You have a lump sum and a mortgage. The lender offers two options, and the difference between them is worth more than most people realise. This guide works through the arithmetic with real numbers, derives the formula behind the shortened term, and sets out exactly what to check before you hand over the money.

1. The two strategies, defined precisely

Both strategies begin identically: you pay a lump sum against the outstanding principal. What happens next is where they diverge, and the divergence is worth real money.

  • Shorten the term — your monthly payment stays exactly as it was. The number of remaining payments falls. You finish years earlier, and the loan ends on a date rather than on a schedule.
  • Reduce the payment — your term stays the same. Your monthly payment is recalculated downward based on the smaller balance. You pay less each month for the same number of months.

Note what is not an option in most standard products: automatically recalculating both. You generally get one or the other, and you must ask for the one you want — some lenders default to reducing the payment because it is administratively simpler.

2. A worked example, in full

Take a loan of 1,000,000 at 4.9% over 30 years. Five years in, you have 200,000 available to prepay. At that point the outstanding balance is approximately 906,000.

Do nothingShorten the termReduce the payment
Balance after prepayment906,000706,000706,000
Monthly payment5,3075,307 (unchanged)about 4,137
Remaining payments300about 176300
Time remaining25 yearsabout 14.7 years25 years
Total interest still to payabout 686,000about 228,000about 535,000
Interest saved vs doing nothing—about 458,000about 151,000

Shortening the term saves roughly three times as much interest as reducing the payment on the same lump sum. The reason is mechanical: with the payment held constant, every month a much larger share goes to principal, and the loan simply stops sooner. With the payment reduced, the smaller balance is stretched over the full remaining term, accruing interest for another 25 years.

3. The mathematics of the shortened term

To shorten the term you hold the payment M fixed and solve the annuity formula for the number of remaining periods n. Starting from:

M = B × r × (1+r)n / [ (1+r)n − 1 ]

where B is the balance after prepayment and r is the monthly rate. Rearranging for n:

n = ln( M / (M − B×r) ) / ln(1 + r)

B = principal after the prepayment  ·  r = monthly rate  ·  M = your unchanged monthly payment

Plugging in the numbers

B = 906,000 − 200,000 = 706,000
r = 0.049 ÷ 12 = 0.0040833
M = 5,307.27 (unchanged)

B × r = 706,000 × 0.0040833 = 2,882.8
M − B×r = 5,307.27 − 2,882.8 = 2,424.5
M / (M − B×r) = 5,307.27 / 2,424.5 = 2.1892
ln(2.1892) = 0.7833  ·  ln(1.0040833) = 0.0040750
n = 0.7833 / 0.0040750 ≈ 192 months  →  about 16 years remaining

Instead of 300 months remaining, you have roughly 192 — you finish about 9 years early, having made the same monthly payment throughout. This is the calculation our prepayment tool performs.

The one constraint that matters

The formula requires M > B × r. If the payment does not exceed the monthly interest on the new balance, the loan never amortises — the payment cannot even cover the interest, and the balance would grow rather than shrink. In practice this is almost never binding on a normal amortising loan, but it is why the calculator must check it rather than assume it.

4. Why timing dominates everything

The same 200,000 prepaid at different points in the loan produces wildly different savings, because what you are really buying is the elimination of future interest — and there is far more future interest early on.

Prepay 200,000 in year…Interest savedMonths removed (shorten term)
Year 2about 640,000about 265
Year 5about 458,000about 108
Year 10about 330,000about 74
Year 15about 215,000about 52

Prepaying in year 2 saves more than twelve times what prepaying in year 25 saves. If you are going to prepay, earlier is dramatically better — and this is also the reason that refinancing into a longer term, which resets you to the top of this table, is so costly.

5. The opportunity cost question

"Should I prepay, or invest the money instead?" is the real decision, and it has a clean answer: compare the two rates after tax and risk.

Prepaying the mortgage earns you a return equal to the loan interest rate — risk-free, tax-free in most jurisdictions, and immediate. A 4.9% mortgage prepaid is a guaranteed 4.9% return.

So the comparison is straightforward:

  • If you can reliably earn more than 4.9% after tax at comparable risk, investing wins on arithmetic.
  • If you can earn less, or the alternative return is uncertain, prepaying wins.
  • If the two are close, the certainty and the psychological benefit of a shorter loan usually tip it toward prepaying.

Be honest about the alternative. A stock market average return is not "reliable" — it is an average of outcomes that include large losses. A savings account at 2% is reliable and loses to a 4.9% mortgage by a wide margin.

6. When reducing the payment is the better choice

Interest saved is not the only objective, and the option with the smaller saving is sometimes correct:

  • Your cash flow is tight or irregular. If your income varies month to month, a lower contractual obligation is genuine insurance.
  • You expect reduced earnings. Parental leave, study, a career change or a planned sabbatical all argue for the lower payment.
  • You would otherwise borrow at a higher rate. This is the decisive one. If keeping the payment high means you will carry a credit card balance at 15–20%, avoiding that is worth far more than optimising a 4.9% mortgage.
  • You want optionality. You can always pay more than the scheduled amount if your circumstances improve; you cannot easily pay less if they worsen. A lower obligation plus voluntary extra payments gives you both.

7. Seven things to check before you prepay

  1. Early repayment penalties. Some loans charge a fee, often a percentage of the amount or a number of months' interest. Others restrict prepayment to specific windows or cap the annual amount. Ask first — a penalty can erase a meaningful part of the saving.
  2. Whether the fee is on the original or outstanding balance. If a penalty is calculated on the original principal rather than the current balance, it can be far larger than you expect in the later years.
  3. Your emergency fund. Do not prepay with money you might need within one to two years. A mortgage is cheap, long-term and effectively irreversible; once the money is in the property, getting it back means borrowing again, on worse terms.
  4. Higher-interest debts. Clear credit card balances and personal loans first. There is no scenario in which prepaying a 4.9% mortgage while carrying an 18% card balance is correct.
  5. Employer retirement matching. An immediate, guaranteed match is usually worth more than the interest you would save. Take the match before prepaying.
  6. Tax treatment. In some jurisdictions mortgage interest is deductible, which reduces the effective benefit of prepaying. Check how it works where you live.
  7. How the lender applies partial payments. Confirm that extra payments go to principal rather than being held against future scheduled payments. Ask in writing.

8. Small, repeated prepayments

You do not need a lump sum. The same principle applies to small extra amounts: each extra unit of principal permanently removes all future interest on it.

Consider rounding the payment up modestly from the very first year on the example loan:

Extra per monthTerm shortened byInterest saved
200about 30 monthsabout 96,000
500about 66 monthsabout 191,000
1,000about 113 monthsabout 297,000
2,000about 176 monthsabout 420,000

An extra 500 a month — less than 10% of the payment — removes more than five years from the schedule and saves roughly 191,000 in interest. Consistency from the early years matters more than the amount.

9. Myths worth discarding

  • "I should wait until the end of the year." No. Every month of delay costs a month of interest on the amount you could have prepaid.
  • "Prepaying hurts my credit score." Closing an account can affect credit mix and age, but paying down an instalment loan generally helps or is neutral. Check before closing the account entirely.
  • "I lose the tax deduction, so it is pointless." A deduction reduces the cost, it does not make borrowing free. Paying 4.9% and deducting part of it still costs more than paying nothing.
  • "I should always prepay." Not if the money has no emergency buffer behind it, or if higher-interest debt exists.
  • "The bank will automatically shorten my term." Usually it will not. You must request it explicitly.

10. A decision sequence

  1. Do you have an emergency fund covering three to six months of essential spending? If not, stop here.
  2. Do you have any debt at a higher rate than the mortgage? If so, clear that first.
  3. Are you capturing any employer retirement match? If not, start.
  4. Does the loan penalise prepayment? Compute the penalty against the saving.
  5. Can you sustain your current payment comfortably? If yes, choose shorten the term. If no, or if your income is uncertain, choose reduce the payment and treat the freed-up cash as a buffer.
  6. Put the decision in writing with the lender, and confirm the new schedule in writing before you transfer anything.
Reference only. All figures are illustrative, computed with standard formulas on the stated assumptions. Prepayment rules, penalties, tax treatment and consumer protections differ substantially between lenders and countries. Read your loan agreement and confirm every figure with your lender before taking action.

提前还款：该缩短期限还是减少月供？

你手上有笔闲钱，同时还有房贷。贷款机构会给你两个选项，两者的差距比多数人想象的要大得多。本文用真实数字完整演算这笔账，推导"缩短期限"背后的公式，并列出在你把钱交出去之前必须逐项确认的清单。

1. 两种策略的精确定义

两种策略的起点完全相同：你用一笔钱冲抵剩余本金。区别发生在之后，而这个区别值真金白银。

  • 缩短期限 —— 月供分文不变。剩余期数减少。你提前数年还清，贷款在一个更早的日期结束。
  • 减少月供 —— 期限不变。按更小的本金重算月供，每月负担下降。同样的期数，每月还得更少。

注意：多数标准产品中不存在的选项是"两者同时重算"。你通常只能二选一，而且必须主动提出你要哪个 —— 部分机构出于操作简便，默认给你"减少月供"。

2. 完整实例演算

贷款 100 万元、年利率 4.9%、期限 30 年。已还 5 年，此时剩余本金约 90.6 万元，你有 20 万元可用于提前还款。

不提前还缩短期限减少月供
还款后本金906,000706,000706,000
月供5,3075,307（不变）约 4,137
剩余期数300约 176300
剩余时间25 年约 14.7 年25 年
后续应付总利息约 686,000约 228,000约 535,000
相比不还款省下—约 458,000约 151,000

同样一笔 20 万元，缩短期限省下的利息约为减少月供的 三倍。原因是机制性的：月供被固定住，意味着每月有更大比例冲抵本金，贷款就直接更早结束；而减少月供，等于把更小的本金摊在完整的剩余期限上，再计 25 年利息。

3. 缩短期限的数学推导

缩短期限，就是保持月供 M 不变，由年金公式反解剩余期数 n。从下式出发：

M = B × r × (1+r)n / [ (1+r)n − 1 ]

其中 B 为提前还款后的本金，r 为月利率。整理后解出 n：

n = ln( M / (M − B×r) ) / ln(1 + r)

B = 提前还款后的本金  ·  r = 月利率  ·  M = 你不变的月供

代入数字

B = 906,000 − 200,000 = 706,000 元
r = 0.049 ÷ 12 = 0.0040833
M = 5,307.27 元（不变）

B × r = 706,000 × 0.0040833 = 2,882.8
M − B×r = 5,307.27 − 2,882.8 = 2,424.5
M / (M − B×r) = 5,307.27 / 2,424.5 = 2.1892
ln(2.1892) = 0.7833  ·  ln(1.0040833) = 0.0040750
n = 0.7833 / 0.0040750 ≈ 192 期  →  约剩 16 年

原本剩余 300 期，现在约 192 期 —— 你提前约 9 年还清，而整个期间月供分文未变。这正是我们提前还款工具所执行的计算。

唯一需要注意的约束

该公式要求 M > B × r。若月供不超过新本金对应的月利息，贷款就无法摊销 —— 月供连利息都覆盖不了，余额会不减反增。实务中对一笔正常摊销的贷款几乎不会触发，但这正是计算工具必须先做判断而不能默认成立的原因。

4. 为什么时点决定一切

同样是 20 万元，在不同年份投入，省下的利息差异极大 —— 因为你真正买下的，是"未来利息的消除"，而早期的未来利息远多于后期。

在第…年还 20 万省下利息缩短期数（缩短期限）
第 2 年约 640,000约 265 期
第 5 年约 458,000约 108 期
第 10 年约 330,000约 74 期
第 15 年约 215,000约 52 期
第 20 年约 120,000约 33 期
第 25 年约 50,000约 17 期

第 2 年还，省下的钱是第 25 年还的十二倍以上。如果要提前还，越早越好 —— 这也正是为什么"为降月供而延长年限"代价高昂：它把你重置回这张表的顶端。

5. 机会成本这道必答题

"该提前还，还是拿去投资？"才是真正的决策，而它有清晰答案：把两个利率放在税后与风险可比的口径上比较。

提前还款给你的回报，等于贷款利率本身 —— 无风险、在多数地区免税、且立即生效。还掉 4.9% 的房贷，就是确定的 4.9% 回报。

于是比较很直接：

  • 若你能稳定拿到高于 4.9% 的税后回报，且风险相当 —— 投资在算术上胜出。
  • 若你能拿到的低于 4.9%，或替代收益并不确定 —— 提前还款胜出。
  • 若两者接近，确定性与"贷款更短"的心理收益通常把天平推向提前还款。

但要对替代方案诚实。股市的平均回报不是"稳定可得"的 —— 它是包含大幅亏损在内的各种结果的平均值。而 2% 的存款是确定的，它输给 4.9% 的房贷，且输得很彻底。

6. 什么情况下"减少月供"更合适

省利息不是唯一目标。省得较少的那个选项，有时才是对的：

  • 现金流紧张或不稳定。若你的收入逐月波动，更低的合约义务本身就是实实在在的保险。
  • 预期收入会下降。育儿、求学、职业转换或计划中的休整期，都支持选更低的月供。
  • 否则你得按更高利率借钱。这是决定性的一条。若维持高月供意味着你要挂着 15~20% 的信用卡账单，那么避免这件事，远比优化 4.9% 的房贷重要得多。
  • 你想要选择权。境况变好时，你随时可以多还；境况变差时，你却很难少还。更低的义务 + 自愿多还，两头都占。

7. 提前还款前必查的七件事

  1. 提前还款违约金。部分贷款收取费用，常按还款金额的百分比或若干个月的利息计。也有贷款限制还款窗口，或设置年度上限。先问 —— 一笔违约金可能吃掉相当部分的节省。
  2. 违约金按原始本金还是剩余本金计。若按原始本金而非当前余额计算，后期这笔费用会远超你的预期。
  3. 应急金。不要拿一两年内可能要用的钱去还款。房贷便宜、期限长，且实际上不可逆 —— 钱一旦进了房子，再拿出来就得重新借，条件更差。
  4. 更高利率的负债。先清信用卡账单与消费贷。在挂着 18% 卡债的同时去还 4.9% 的房贷，没有任何一种情形是合理的。
  5. 雇主养老金匹配。一笔即时、确定的匹配，通常比你省下的利息更值钱。先拿满匹配，再谈提前还款。
  6. 税务处理。在部分地区房贷利息可抵税，这会削弱提前还款的实际收益。查清你所在地的规则。
  7. 机构如何处置部分还款。确认额外还款是冲抵本金，还是被当作预存用于抵扣未来期款。要求书面确认。

8. 小额、多次还款的效果

不一定要攒够一大笔。同样的原理适用于小额额外还款：每多还一单位本金，就永久消除它未来的全部利息。

仍以上例贷款，从第一年起把月供向上小幅取整：

每月多还缩短期数省下利息
200 元约 30 期约 96,000
500 元约 66 期约 191,000
1,000 元约 113 期约 297,000
2,000 元约 176 期约 420,000

每月多还 500 元 —— 不到月供的 10% —— 就能把还款计划缩短五年以上，省下约 19.1 万元利息。早期开始的持续性，比金额本身更重要。

9. 值得丢掉的几个误区

  • "等到年底再还。"不对。每拖一个月，就多付一个月本可省下的利息。
  • "提前还款会伤信用分。"结清账户可能影响信用组合与账户年限，但偿还分期贷款通常有利或中性。彻底销户前先确认。
  • "抵税没了就没意义。"抵税只是降低成本，不会让借款变成免费。付 4.9% 再抵扣一部分，仍然比不付更贵。
  • "永远都该提前还。"若这笔钱背后没有应急垫，或存在更高息负债，就不该。
  • "银行会自动给我缩短期限。"通常不会。你必须明确提出。

10. 决策顺序

  1. 你有覆盖 3~6 个月必要开支的应急金吗？没有就到此为止。
  2. 你有任何利率高于房贷的负债吗？有就先清掉。
  3. 你拿满雇主的养老金匹配了吗？没有就先开始。
  4. 这笔贷款对提前还款收违约金吗？把违约金和节省额摆在一起算。
  5. 你能轻松维持当前月供吗？能 → 选缩短期限；不能，或收入不确定 → 选减少月供，把释放出来的现金当作缓冲。
  6. 与贷款机构书面确认，并在划款前拿到新的还款计划书面确认。
仅供参考。所有数字均为在给定假设下、用标准公式计算的示例。提前还款规则、违约金、税务处理与消费者保护在不同机构与国家之间差异很大。请阅读你的贷款合同，并在行动前向贷款机构核实每一个数字。
