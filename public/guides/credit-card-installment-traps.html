Credit Card Instalments: Why "0.6% per Month" Is Really About 13% a Year

"Convert your purchase into 12 easy instalments at just 0.6% per month." It sounds cheap: 0.6 × 12 is 7.2%, which feels manageable. The true cost is closer to 13% a year. This guide explains precisely why, derives the calculation so you can reproduce it yourself, and sets out the traps that make instalments more expensive than they appear.

1. The gap between the advertised number and the real one

An instalment plan quoted at "0.6% per month" invites a simple mental calculation: 0.6 × 12 = 7.2% per year. That number is wrong, and it is wrong by nearly a factor of two.

The reason is not hidden fees or fine print. It is a single structural feature: the fee is charged on the original amount, while the balance you owe shrinks every month. Once you see that, the doubling is obvious.

2. The mechanism: a fee that never shrinks

On a typical instalment plan of 12,000 over 12 months at 0.6% per month:

Monthly fee = 12,000 × 0.6% = 72, charged every month without change
Monthly principal = 12,000 ÷ 12 = 1,000
Total monthly payment = 1,072

Total paid = 1,072 × 12 = 12,864
Total fee = 864

Now look at what happens to the effective rate on the money you actually still owe:

MonthBalance owed at startFee chargedFee as % of balance owed

In the final month you owe 1,000 and pay a 72 fee — that is 7.2% for a single month, which annualises to well over 80%. Conversely, in the first month the fee on the amount you are actually using is only 0.6%. Averaged across the year, you had use of roughly half the money (you borrowed 12,000 at the start and zero at the end), but you paid a full-year fee on all of it. That is the entire trick.

3. Computing the real rate: the IRR method

The honest measure of any repayment schedule is its internal rate of return — the interest rate that makes the present value of all your payments exactly equal to the amount you received. Solve for the monthly rate r in:

P = ∑k=1..n   payment / (1 + r)k

then   APR ≈ r × 12

P = amount financed  ·  payment = your fixed monthly payment  ·  n = number of months

There is no closed-form solution for r when n is large; it is found by iteration. A bisection search works well: try r = 0 (where the present value of payments exceeds P) and r = 100% (where it is far below), then repeatedly test the midpoint and keep the half that brackets the answer. Fifty to a hundred iterations give more precision than you need.

The result for our example

Payment = 1,072 per month for 12 months, P = 12,000
Solving gives r ≈ 1.086% per month
APR = 1.086% × 12 = 13.03% per year

Advertised figure: 7.2%  ·  True figure: 13.03%

The true cost is about 1.8 times the advertised annual figure. This is the calculation our APR tool performs.

4. A rule of thumb you can use without a calculator

For a plan with a constant fee on the original balance, the true annual rate is approximately:

APR ≈ 2 × monthly fee rate × 12 × n / (n + 1)

where n is the number of months. The factor n/(n+1) approaches 1 as the term lengthens, so:

  • 3 months: 2 × 0.6% × 12 × 3/4 = 10.8%
  • 12 months: 2 × 0.6% × 12 × 12/13 = 13.3% (close to the exact 13.03%)
  • 24 months: 2 × 0.6% × 12 × 24/25 = 13.8%

Memorise this: the true annual rate is roughly double the advertised annual rate. A "0.6% per month" plan is a 13% loan. A "0.5% per month" plan is about 11%.

5. Comparing terms on the same purchase

Notice the shape of this. The monthly payment falls sharply as the term lengthens, which is the number the advertisement shows you. The true APR barely moves. The total fee roughly doubles from 12 to 24 months. So the choice of term is essentially a choice about how much total fee you pay, not about the rate — and shorter is cheaper in total.

6. Instalment plans versus revolving balances

These are different products and it matters which one you are in:

Instalment planRevolving balance
StructureFixed number of payments, then doneOpen-ended; you choose how much to pay
CostA fee, often quoted as a monthly rateInterest on the daily balance, quoted as APR
Typical rate10–14% true APR15–22% APR in many markets
End dateKnownUnknown if you pay the minimum
Grace periodUsually none on the instalmentLost once you carry a balance

An instalment at 13% is usually cheaper than revolving at 18–20%. Converting a balance you were going to revolve into an instalment is often a genuine improvement. The mistake is taking an instalment on a purchase you could otherwise have paid off in full, where the alternative cost was zero.

7. The minimum-payment trap, quantified

If you carry 20,000 on a revolving balance at 18% APR and pay only the minimum — commonly 2% of the balance or a small fixed amount, whichever is greater — the repayment stretches for many years and the total interest can exceed the original purchase. The exact figures depend on the minimum formula, but the shape of the outcome does not: minimum payments are designed to keep you paying, not to get you out.

If you are only paying the minimum, the single most valuable change is not switching products — it is paying more than the minimum. Even a modest fixed extra amount, applied consistently, shortens the repayment dramatically.

8. Six traps to check for

  1. Fee charged upfront and non-refundable. Some plans debit the entire fee immediately. If you settle early, you may receive no refund of the unearned portion. Ask what happens if you clear the balance in month 3 of 12.
  2. Early settlement penalties. Some plans charge the remaining fees, or a flat penalty, if you pay off early. This removes your ability to fix the mistake.
  3. Fee calculated on the original balance. This is the standard structure and the reason for the doubling. Some products calculate on the outstanding balance instead — those are genuinely cheaper and worth seeking out. Always ask which applies.
  4. Fees that start after a promotional period. A "0% for 6 months" offer may convert to a high rate, sometimes applied retroactively, if not cleared in time. Read the conversion terms.
  5. Consumption of your credit limit. An instalment typically occupies your available credit for the full term, which can push your utilisation up and affect your score even while you pay on time.
  6. Interaction with the grace period. Adding an instalment does not restore a grace period you have lost by revolving. Understand which part of your balance is accruing interest immediately.

9. When an instalment plan is still a reasonable choice

  • Genuine zero-fee promotions — if there is truly no fee and no interest, it is free short-term credit. Confirm in writing, and check what happens if you miss a payment.
  • The alternative is revolving at a higher rate — a 13% instalment beats an 18% revolving balance.
  • A necessary expense with a timing mismatch — spreading a genuinely needed purchase can be rational, provided you have priced the cost honestly and can sustain the payments.
  • You need the cash for something with a higher return — rare, and it should be a real return, not a hoped-for one.

10. A checklist before accepting any instalment offer

  • Compute the true APR, not the advertised rate. Our calculator does this from the fee rate and term.
  • Compute the total fee in currency, and decide whether that amount is acceptable for the convenience.
  • Ask whether the fee is on the original or outstanding balance.
  • Ask what happens if you settle early.
  • Check the effect on your available credit and utilisation.
  • Confirm you can sustain the payment alongside all other obligations — add it to your total debt ratio, not to your memory.
  • Read whether the offer converts to a different rate after any promotional period.
Reference only. Calculations here use the IRR method on a constant-fee schedule, which is the standard approach but not the only one. Issuers may calculate, disclose and round costs differently, and consumer protection rules vary by country. Always read your card agreement and confirm figures with your issuer.

信用卡分期：为什么"月费率 0.6%"实际年化接近 13%

"12 期分期，月费率只要 0.6%。"听起来很便宜：0.6 × 12 = 7.2%，似乎可以接受。但真实成本接近年化 13%，差了将近一倍。本文精确解释为什么会这样，推导出你可以自己复现的算法，并列出让分期比表面更贵的那些陷阱。

1. 宣传数字与真实数字之间的鸿沟

一个标为"月费率 0.6%"的分期方案，会引导你做一个简单的心算：0.6 × 12 = 年化 7.2%。这个数字是错的，而且错了将近一倍。

原因不在于隐藏费用或条款细则，而在于一个结构性特征：手续费按原始金额收取，而你欠的钱逐月在减少。一旦看清这一点，"翻倍"就显而易见了。

2. 机制：一笔永不缩水的手续费

以 12,000 元分 12 期、月费率 0.6% 为例：

每月手续费 = 12,000 × 0.6% = 72 元，月月如此、分文不变
每月本金 = 12,000 ÷ 12 = 1,000 元
每月还款合计 = 1,072 元

累计还款 = 1,072 × 12 = 12,864 元
总手续费 = 864 元

现在看这笔手续费相对于"你实际还欠多少"的比率如何变化：

期数期初尚欠当期手续费占尚欠余额比例
第 1 期12,000720.60%
第 3 期10,000720.72%
第 6 期7,000721.03%
第 9 期4,000721.80%
第 11 期2,000723.60%
第 12 期1,000727.20%

最后一期你只欠 1,000 元，却仍付 72 元手续费 —— 单月 7.2%，年化超过 80%。反过来，第一期你实际占用全部 12,000 元，72 元只占 0.6%。全年平均，你大约只用了一半的钱（期初借用 12,000，期末归零），却按全额付了一整年费用。这就是全部的玄机。

3. 计算真实利率：IRR 法

衡量任何还款方案诚实的指标是它的内部收益率（IRR）—— 使你所有还款的现值恰好等于你拿到手金额的那个利率。解出月利率 r：

P = ∑k=1..n   每期还款 / (1 + r)k

则   APR ≈ r × 12

P = 分期本金  ·  每期还款 = 你的固定月供  ·  n = 期数

当 n 较大时，r 没有闭式解，需迭代求解。二分法很好用：先试 r = 0（此时还款现值大于 P）和 r = 100%（此时远小于 P），然后反复取中点测试，保留夹住答案的那半区间。迭代 50~100 次，精度已远超需要。

上例的计算结果

每期还款 = 1,072 元 × 12 期，P = 12,000 元
解得 r ≈ 1.086% / 月
APR = 1.086% × 12 = 年化 13.03%

宣传口径：7.2%  ·  真实口径：13.03%

真实成本约为宣传年化数字的 1.8 倍。这正是我们信用卡 APR 工具所执行的计算。

4. 不用计算器也能用的口诀

对"按原始本金恒定收费"的分期，真实年化约等于：

APR ≈ 2 × 月费率 × 12 × n / (n + 1)

其中 n 为期数。因子 n/(n+1) 随期限变长趋近于 1，于是：

  • 3 期：2 × 0.6% × 12 × 3/4 = 10.8%
  • 12 期：2 × 0.6% × 12 × 12/13 = 13.3%（与精确的 13.03% 很接近）
  • 24 期：2 × 0.6% × 12 × 24/25 = 13.8%

请记住这句：真实年化约为宣传年化数字的两倍。"月费率 0.6%"就是一笔 13% 的贷款；"月费率 0.5%"约为 11%。

5. 同一笔消费，不同期数横向对比

同样是 12,000 元、月费率 0.6%，只改变期数：

期数月供总手续费占消费额比例真实年化
3 期4,0722161.8%约 10.8%
6 期2,0724323.6%约 12.2%

注意这张表的形状：月供随期限显著下降，而这正是广告展示给你的那个数字；真实年化几乎不动；总手续费从 12 期到 24 期大约翻倍。所以选择期数，本质上是在选择"总手续费付多少"，而不是在选利率 —— 且期限越短，总额越便宜。

6. 分期与循环账单：两种不同产品

这是两种不同的产品，你身处哪一种很关键：

分期计划循环账单
结构固定期数，还完即止开放式，还多少由你定
计价手续费，常以月费率表述按日计息，以年化利率表述
常见水平真实年化 10~14%多数市场 15~22%
结束时间明确只还最低还款则无期
免息期分期部分通常没有一旦挂账即整体失效

13% 的分期通常比 18~20% 的循环便宜。把一笔你本来就要挂着的账单转成分期，往往是实打实的改善。真正的错误在于：对一笔你本可以一次性还清的消费去办分期 —— 那种情况下替代成本是零。

7. 最低还款陷阱，量化来看

若你挂着 20,000 元循环账单、年利率 18%，且只还最低还款额（常见为余额的 2% 或一个小额固定值，取较高者），还款会拖上很多年，总利息甚至可能超过消费本金本身。具体数字取决于最低还款的计算公式，但结果的形状不变：最低还款额的设计目的是让你一直还，而不是让你还清。

如果你现在只还最低还款额，最有价值的改变不是换产品，而是多还一点。哪怕只是固定多还一小笔、且持续坚持，也能把还款周期大幅缩短。

8. 六个必须确认的坑

  1. 手续费一次性收取、提前结清不退。部分方案在首期即扣全额手续费。若你提前结清，未发生部分可能不予退还。要问清：12 期的分期在第 3 期还清会怎样。
  2. 提前结清违约金。部分方案提前还清时收取剩余手续费或固定罚金。这直接拿掉了你纠正错误的机会。
  3. 手续费按原始本金计。这是标准结构，也正是"翻倍"的成因。也有产品按剩余本金计费 —— 那些确实更便宜，值得优先选择。一定问清是哪一种。
  4. 优惠期结束后才开收的费用。"前 6 期 0 费率"可能在到期后转为高利率，有时甚至具有追溯性。务必读清转换条款。
  5. 占用信用额度。分期通常在整个期间占用你的可用额度。即便你按时还款，也可能推高使用率、影响评分。
  6. 与免息期的相互作用。增加一笔分期，并不会恢复你因挂账而失去的免息期。要弄清余额中哪一部分正在即时计息。

9. 什么情况下分期仍然合理

  • 真正的零费率活动 —— 若确实无息无费，那就是免费的短期资金。要有书面确认，并查清逾期会怎样。
  • 替代方案是更高利率的循环 —— 13% 的分期优于 18% 的循环账单。
  • 必要支出与时点错配 —— 把一笔真实需要的支出摊开可以是理性的，前提是你诚实地给它定了价，且撑得住月供。
  • 手上的现金有更高回报用途 —— 这种情况很少，而且那个回报必须是真实的，而不是你希望中的。

10. 接受任何分期方案前的清单

  • 算清真实年化，而不是看宣传费率。我们的计算器可按费率与期数直接算出。
  • 算清总手续费的绝对金额，并判断为这份便利付这笔钱是否值得。
  • 问清手续费是按原始本金还是剩余本金计。
  • 问清提前结清会怎样。
  • 检查对可用额度与使用率的影响。
  • 确认你能同时负担这笔月供与所有其他负债 —— 把它加进总负债比，而不是只记在脑子里。
  • 看清优惠期结束后是否会转换为另一种费率。
仅供参考。本文计算采用恒定收费方案下的 IRR 法，这是标准做法但不是唯一做法。发卡行在计算、披露与进位上可能采用不同口径，消费者保护规则也因国家而异。请阅读你的信用卡协议，并向发卡行核实具体数字。
