Exchange Rates Explained: Mid-Market, Bank Rates and the Hidden Spread

Published October 22, 2026 · 7 min read · By CalcHub Pro

You look up a rate, convert your money, and receive noticeably less than expected. The rate was not wrong — you were looking at a different rate. Understanding which rate you are seeing explains most of the gap.

1. Three different rates for the same currency pair

Mid-market rate

The midpoint between the buying and selling prices in the interbank market — essentially the "wholesale" rate. This is the number you see on search engines and rate APIs. Almost no individual consumer can actually transact at this rate. Our converter uses it as a reference.

Bank or provider rate

What your bank or exchange provider offers you. It is the mid-market rate plus a margin (the spread), which is how they earn. Consumer margins commonly range from roughly 1% to 5% depending on currency, amount and channel.

Card network rate

When you pay by card abroad, the network (Visa, Mastercard and similar) sets a wholesale rate, and your issuer may add its own foreign transaction fee on top — often 1–3%.

2. Where the money actually goes

Converting 1,000 at a mid-market rate of 7.20 might suggest 7,200 in return. In practice you receive less, because:

  • Spread: the provider's buy/sell margin, typically 1–3% for major currencies, more for exotic ones.
  • Fixed fee: a flat charge per transaction, which hurts small transfers disproportionately.
  • Commission: a percentage fee at some bureaux, sometimes advertised as "0% commission" while the spread is wider.
  • Intermediary or receiving bank charges: especially on international wires, where fees can be deducted en route.
  • ATM operator fees when withdrawing cash abroad.

3. How to judge whether a rate is fair

Get the mid-market rate first — that is your benchmark. Then ask the provider for the final amount the recipient will receive, after all fees. The effective cost is:

Effective cost % = (mid-market amount − amount actually received) ÷ mid-market amount × 100

Any provider unwilling to state the final received amount is a warning sign. "Zero commission" and "no fees" usually means the cost has been moved into the exchange rate.

4. Rounding and presentation tricks

Consumer rates are often quoted with fewer decimal places than the wholesale market, and rounding is not always in your favour. Small differences look trivial on one unit but scale: on a large transfer, a 0.5% difference is substantial.

5. Why rates move

Currency prices respond to interest-rate differentials, inflation data, economic growth, political and policy developments, and market sentiment. Rates move continuously during the trading week and are typically stable but untraded over the weekend, which is why some providers add a weekend margin to cover the risk of a gap when markets reopen.

6. Practical tips

  • Compare the final amount received, never the headline rate.
  • Avoid airport and hotel exchange desks, which typically carry the widest margins.
  • When paying by card abroad, decline dynamic currency conversion — if a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, that rate is usually worse than your network's. Choose the local currency.
  • Combine transfers where a fixed fee applies, since one large transfer costs less proportionally than several small ones.
  • Check the timestamp. A rate is only meaningful at the moment it was quoted.

汇率详解：中间价、银行报价与隐藏的点差

发布于 2026 年 10 月 22 日 · 阅读约 7 分钟 · CalcHub Pro

你查了一个汇率，换完钱却发现到手的明显比预期少。汇率并没有错 —— 你看的是另一个汇率。弄清自己看到的是哪一种，就能解释绝大部分差额。

1. 同一货币对的三种汇率

中间价（Mid-market rate）

银行间市场买入价与卖出价的中点，基本等同于"批发价"。这是你在搜索引擎和汇率接口上看到的数字。几乎没有个人消费者能真正按这个价格成交。我们的换算工具把它作为参考价。

银行/服务商报价

你的银行或换汇机构给你的价格，等于中间价加上点差，这是他们的盈利来源。对主要货币，个人客户的点差常见在 1%~5% 之间，取决于币种、金额与渠道。

卡组织汇率

境外刷卡时，卡组织（Visa、Mastercard 等）给出批发汇率，你的发卡行可能再叠加一笔境外交易费，常见 1~3%。

2. 钱到底去哪了

按中间价 7.20 把 1,000 换出去，似乎应得 7,200。实际到手更少，因为：

  • 点差：服务商的买卖差价，主要币种通常 1~3%，小币种更高。
  • 固定手续费：每笔固定收费，对小额换汇的伤害尤其大。
  • 佣金：部分兑换点按百分比收费；有些标榜"零佣金"，但点差更宽。
  • 中转行/收款行费用：国际电汇尤其常见，途中可能被扣费。
  • ATM 运营方手续费（境外取现时）。

3. 如何判断一个汇率是否公道

先拿到中间价 —— 这是你的基准。然后向服务商询问：扣除所有费用后，收款方实际到账多少。实际成本为：

实际成本 % =（按中间价应得金额 − 实际到账金额）÷ 按中间价应得金额 × 100

任何不愿说明最终到账金额的服务商，都是一个警示信号。"零佣金""免手续费"通常意味着成本被挪进了汇率里。

4. 报价位数与进位技巧

面向个人的报价，小数位数常少于批发市场，且进位方向未必对你有利。单个单位上的微小差异看起来不起眼，但会随金额放大：大额换汇时 0.5% 的差距相当可观。

5. 汇率为什么会动

汇率受利率差、通胀数据、经济增长、政治与政策变化，以及市场情绪影响。交易日内持续波动；周末市场通常休市，价格虽稳但无法成交 —— 这也是部分服务商对周末交易加收点差的原因，用来对冲开市跳空的风险。

6. 实用建议

  • 比较最终到账金额，而不是宣传汇率。
  • 避开机场与酒店的兑换柜台，点差通常最宽。
  • 境外刷卡时拒绝动态货币转换（DCC） —— 若终端询问是否以你的本币结算，那个汇率通常劣于卡组织汇率。请选择当地货币。
  • 合并换汇：存在固定手续费时，一笔大额比多笔小额的单位成本更低。
  • 看时间戳。汇率只在报价的那一刻有意义。
