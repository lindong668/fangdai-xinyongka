China Property Purchase Taxes: Deed Tax, VAT and What Buyers Forget

The listed price of a Chinese home is not what you pay. Deed tax, VAT, income tax and a handful of smaller charges sit on top — and which of them apply depends on two numbers: the floor area and how long the seller has held the property.

1. The sticker price is not the price

On second-hand transactions the buyer routinely pays well above the agreed price. Some of these taxes are legally the seller's obligation but are, in practice, negotiated into the buyer's side of the deal. Knowing which is which is the difference between budgeting correctly and running short at settlement.

2. Deed tax: the 140㎡ line

Deed tax (契税) is the buyer's tax, and the threshold that decides the rate is floor area. Under the policy in force since 1 December 2024:

PropertyArea ≤ 140㎡Area > 140㎡
Family's only home1%1.5%
Family's second home1%2%
Third or subsequent3%
The threshold was raised from 90㎡ to 140㎡. The 90㎡ standard came from a 2016 circular that has since been repealed. A 95㎡ first home is now squarely inside the 1% band — using the old 90㎡ threshold overstates the tax by half.

On a ¥3,000,000 first home of 95㎡, deed tax is ¥30,000 — not ¥45,000.

3. VAT: the two-year rule

VAT (增值税) on a resale depends on how long the seller has held the property. The ordinary-residence distinction has been removed, so the rule is now uniform nationwide:

  • Held 2 years or more: exempt.
  • Held under 2 years: levied on the full price at a 3% levy rate, i.e. price ÷ 1.03 × 3% — an effective 2.91% of the sale price.

On a ¥3,000,000 sale held under two years, that is ¥87,379. Note it is charged on the full price, not on the seller's gain.

4. Income tax: the 满五唯一 exemption

Personal income tax (个税) on a property sale is either 20% of the gain or 1% of the full price, whichever the local authority applies. It is waived entirely when the property is the seller's only home and has been held for five years or more — the 满五唯一 condition.

On a ¥3,000,000 sale with a ¥2,000,000 original cost: 20% of the ¥1,000,000 gain is ¥200,000, versus ¥30,000 at the 1% flat rate. Which method applies changes the bill by a factor of nearly seven, so confirm it with the local tax office before budgeting.

5. A worked example

First home, second-hand, ¥3,000,000, 95㎡, seller has held it under two years and it is not their only property:

ChargeAmountBasis

Had the seller held it two years or more, VAT disappears and the total falls to ¥260,000. Had it also been their only home for five years, income tax disappears too and only the ¥30,000 deed tax remains. The holding period is worth more than any negotiation on price.

6. The costs nobody quotes

  • Agency commission (中介费) — commonly 1% to 2% of the price, sometimes split, sometimes entirely the buyer's.
  • Maintenance fund (维修基金) — applies to new builds, charged per square metre.
  • Valuation and registration fees — small individually, but they add up.
  • Mortgage registration fee — a fixed charge for registering the lender's security interest.

7. How to estimate your own total

You need five inputs: price, area, whether it is the family's first or second home, how long the seller has held it, and whether the 满五唯一 condition is met. The first three set deed tax; the last two decide whether VAT and income tax apply at all.

8. The full deed tax table

Under the policy in force since December 2024, deed tax on housing depends on two things only — whether the purchase is the household's first or second home, and whether the floor area is above 140㎡:

140㎡ or lessAbove 140㎡
First home1%1.5%
Second home1%2%

Note that the second-home rate is identical to the first-home rate below the threshold. Above 140㎡ it is 2%, half a point above the first-home rate. Third and subsequent homes, and non-residential property, generally do not qualify for the reduced rates and are taxed at the statutory rate set by the local authority — commonly 3%.

The 140㎡ line replaced the old 90㎡ standard. A 95㎡ first home is now squarely inside the 1% band. Using the old threshold would overstate the tax by half.

9. VAT: how the two-year test works

Since 1 January 2026, the rule for an individual selling a home is uniform nationwide and no longer distinguishes ordinary from non-ordinary housing:

  • Held two years or more: exempt entirely.
  • Held under two years: VAT at a 3% levy rate on the full price, computed as price ÷ 1.03 × 3% — an effective 2.91% of the sale price.

Two details catch people out. First, it is charged on the full price, not on the gain — a property sold at a loss still incurs it. Second, the two-year clock is generally measured from the date on the ownership certificate or the tax invoice, not from the date you moved in. Confirm which document applies locally.

The levy rate was reduced from 5% to 3% with effect from 1 January 2026. Guidance written before that date overstates the tax substantially.

10. Income tax: two methods and one exemption

On a resale, individual income tax is computed either as 20% of the gain or as a flat percentage of the full price, depending on local practice. It is waived entirely where the property was the seller's only home and has been held for five years or more.

On a ¥3,000,000 sale with a ¥2,000,000 original cost, the gain is ¥1,000,000:

  • 20% of the gain: ¥200,000
  • Flat 1% of the full price: ¥30,000
  • With the five-year-only-home exemption: ¥0

The difference between methods is nearly sevenfold, and the exemption can remove the tax entirely. Establish which applies before agreeing a price.

11. The charges that sit outside the tax bill

  • Agency commission. Usually a percentage of the price, and frequently the largest single non-tax cost.
  • Maintenance fund. Levied on new builds, typically by floor area.
  • Registration and survey fees. Small individually but not optional.
  • Notarisation and certification. Required in some transactions and cities.

Together these commonly add 1% to 3% of the price on top of the taxes above. They are rarely quoted in the headline figure.

12. Do the seller's taxes end up on the buyer?

Legally the seller bears VAT and income tax; in practice the net price is negotiated, so the economic burden depends on the market. In a strong market these costs are frequently passed through to the buyer; in a weak one the seller absorbs them.

What matters for budgeting is the total, not the legal incidence. Ask for the all-in figure and compare properties on that basis.

13. Checking the current position

Housing transaction taxes have changed several times in recent years — the deed tax threshold moved to 140㎡ in December 2024, and the VAT levy rate on short-held sales fell from 5% to 3% on 1 January 2026. Local authorities also set detailed implementation rules.

  • Confirm rates with the local tax authority before budgeting.
  • Check which document starts the two-year clock in your city.
  • Ask whether any current purchase subsidy or relief applies to your transaction.

An estimate built on a repealed circular can be wrong by tens of thousands of yuan — which is exactly why the threshold and the rate are worth verifying rather than assuming.
