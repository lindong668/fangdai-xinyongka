How Mortgages Actually Work: Principal, Interest and Amortisation

Published October 22, 2026 · 8 min read · By CalcHub Pro

Most people know their monthly payment and almost nothing else about their loan. That gap is expensive: without knowing how a mortgage amortises, you cannot tell whether an early repayment is worth it, or what a 1% rate rise will do to your budget. This guide explains the mechanics in plain language.

1. The two repayment structures

Equal monthly instalment (annuity / 等额本息)

You pay the same amount every month for the whole term. Early on, most of each payment is interest and only a little reduces the principal; over time that ratio flips. The formula:

M = P × r × (1+r)n / [ (1+r)n − 1 ]
M = monthly payment · P = principal · r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12) · n = total number of months

Example: 1,000,000 at 4.9% over 30 years gives a monthly payment of about 5,307, and total interest of roughly 911,000 — nearly as much again as the loan itself.

Equal principal (等额本金)

Each month you repay the same slice of principal, plus interest on whatever is still outstanding. The first payment is the highest and the last is the lowest. Total interest is lower — in the example above about 737,000 versus 911,000 — but the early payments are significantly heavier, which matters for cash flow.

2. Why the early years are mostly interest

Interest each month equals the outstanding balance times the monthly rate. At the start the balance is at its maximum, so interest is at its maximum. With 1,000,000 at 4.9%, the first month's interest alone is about 4,083 — which is why only around 1,200 of a 5,307 payment actually reduces what you owe in month one.

This is also why prepaying early saves far more than prepaying late: you eliminate years of interest on that principal, not months.

3. Fixed versus variable rates

A fixed rate gives certainty but usually starts higher. A variable rate follows a benchmark (such as an LPR or prime rate plus or minus a margin) and can move. The right question is not "which is cheaper today?" but "what happens to my budget if rates rise by 1 or 2 points?" A stress test answers that: on the example loan, a 1-point rise raises the monthly payment by roughly 585.

4. Debt-to-income: the ratio lenders watch

Two thresholds are widely used. The housing ratio (housing payment ÷ gross income) is generally considered comfortable at or below 28%. The total debt ratio (all debt payments ÷ gross income) is generally considered comfortable at or below 36%, with 43% often treated as an upper bound. These are conventions, not laws, and lenders vary — but they are a useful reality check before you commit.

5. Fees and costs beyond the rate

The interest rate is only part of the cost. Watch for origination or arrangement fees, valuation and legal fees, mandatory insurance, and any penalty for early repayment. A loan with a slightly lower rate and high upfront fees can cost more than the alternative if you repay early.

6. Should you repay early?

Do the arithmetic before deciding. Compare two strategies:

  • Shorten the term — keep the payment the same, finish years sooner. Usually saves the most interest.
  • Reduce the payment — keep the term, lower the monthly burden. Saves less but frees up monthly cash flow.

Prepayment makes most sense when your loan rate is clearly higher than what you could safely earn elsewhere, and when you already hold an emergency fund. If your employer matches retirement contributions, that match usually beats the interest saved.

房贷到底怎么算：本金、利息与摊销

发布于 2026 年 10 月 22 日 · 阅读约 8 分钟 · CalcHub Pro

多数人只知道自己每月还多少，对贷款的其他部分一无所知。这个信息差是有代价的：不了解摊销机制，就无法判断提前还款是否划算，也算不出利率上升 1% 会对你的现金流造成什么影响。本文用通俗语言讲清这套机制。

1. 两种还款结构

等额本息

整个期限内每月还款额相同。前期月供中大部分是利息、只有少部分冲抵本金，后期这个比例逐渐反转。公式如下：

M = P × r × (1+r)n / [ (1+r)n − 1 ]
M = 月供 · P = 本金 · r = 月利率（年利率 ÷ 12） · n = 总期数（月）

举例：100 万元、年利率 4.9%、30 年，月供约 5,307 元，总利息约 91.1 万元 —— 几乎接近本金本身。

等额本金

每月偿还相同的本金，加上剩余本金的利息。首月最高、末月最低。总利息更低 —— 上例约 73.7 万元，对比等额本息的 91.1 万元 —— 但前期月供明显更重，这对现金流是个考验。

2. 为什么前期几乎都在还利息

每月利息 = 剩余本金 × 月利率。初期本金最大，因此利息也最大。100 万元、4.9% 的情况下，首月利息就约 4,083 元 —— 这就是为什么 5,307 元的月供里，第一个月只有约 1,200 元真正减少了你的欠款。

这也解释了为什么早还比晚还划算得多：你省掉的是这笔本金未来数年的利息，而非数月。

3. 固定利率与浮动利率

固定利率带来确定性，但通常起点更高。浮动利率挂钩某个基准（如 LPR 或最优贷款利率加减点），会随市场变动。真正该问的不是"现在哪个更便宜"，而是"利率涨 1~2 个点，我的预算撑得住吗"。压力测试能回答这个问题：上例中利率上升 1 个点，月供约增加 585 元。

4. 负债收入比：贷款机构盯的指标

常用两个阈值：住房负担比（住房支出 ÷ 税前收入）一般建议不超过 28%；总负债比（全部负债月供 ÷ 税前收入）一般建议不超过 36%，43% 常被视为上限。这些是行业惯例而非法律规定，各家机构口径不同 —— 但在你签字前，这是个有用的现实检验。

5. 利率之外的成本

利率只是成本的一部分。还要留意：手续费/安排费、评估费与法律费用、强制保险，以及提前还款违约金。如果一笔贷款利率略低但前期费用很高，而你又打算提前还款，它可能反而更贵。

6. 该不该提前还款？

先算再决定。对比两种策略：

  • 缩短期限 —— 月供不变，提前数年还清。通常最省利息。
  • 减少月供 —— 期限不变，降低每月负担。省得较少，但释放月度现金流。

提前还款最划算的情形是：你的贷款利率明显高于你能安全获得的替代收益，且你已经备好应急金。如果雇主为你的养老金缴费提供匹配，那份匹配通常优于你省下的利息。

仅供参考。本文数字为使用标准公式的示例。实际条款取决于贷款机构、所在地司法管辖与你的信用状况。作出决定前请向贷款机构核实。
